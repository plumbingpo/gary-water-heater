Not every water heater job is a straight replacement. Plenty of Gary, IN projects start with a finished basement or a new addition that needs its own heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. All of them mean new gas piping, vent routes, circuits, or water lines, planned out before anyone cuts a pipe. We take on installations of every size, from a small point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Sizing and placement decide most of the outcome. For a tank, we compare first-hour ratings against your household's busiest hour; for a tankless unit, we size by flow rate and winter temperature rise. Location comes next. Gas heaters need a proper vent route and combustion air, heat pump heaters need open space and a condensate drain, and whole-house electric tankless units often need a large dedicated circuit and the panel capacity to match. Put a heater above a finished ceiling and it needs a drain pan; put a gas unit in a garage and code may require a raised platform. You get the trade-offs in plain terms, with no pressure.

On installation day, every step gets verified. We run and pressure-test new gas piping, add a sediment trap, and route venting exactly to spec. Electric and heat pump units get a circuit matched to their load. Water lines go in with accessible shutoffs and proper transitions between dissimilar metals, and the relief valve is piped full size to a safe discharge point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Warning Signs to Watch For

Most tanks announce trouble weeks or months ahead with rumbling noises, rusty water, or hot showers that keep getting shorter. These are the red flags our technicians see most often before a breakdown:

  • A new bathroom, addition, or finished basement that needs more hot water
  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore
  • A remodel that moves the utility space to a new spot
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • A tank in a tight spot that is hard to service or replace
  • Interest in tankless or heat pump efficiency
  • Gallons wasted down the drain while you wait for hot water

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Installation

A new water heater installation is really several trades in one job. The gas piping must be sized for the heater plus every other appliance on the line, then pressure-tested until it is leak-free. Venting has to follow the manufacturer's rules for diameter, length, pitch, and clearance to combustibles, or carbon monoxide can spill back into the house. Electric and heat pump units need a circuit, breaker, and wire gauge matched to their load, and whole-house electric tankless units can draw more current than many panels have to spare. On the water side, there is more: shutoffs, dielectric transitions, a correctly rated relief valve with a full-size discharge line, and code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps that vary by location.

We test every joint, check every gas install for draft and carbon monoxide, and set the temperature low enough to prevent scalding. Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Start with these practical measures our team relies on:

  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Flush a tank every six to twelve months to keep sediment from settling
  • Nothing flammable belongs near a gas burner or its air supply
  • Tankless heaters need a yearly flush to clear scale from the heat exchanger
  • Keep the temperature around 120 degrees; hotter settings raise the risk of scalding
  • Moisture in the drain pan or at the relief line is an early warning worth a call
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
